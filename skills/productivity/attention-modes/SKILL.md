# Attention Modes

Use **Focused** when the user is available for a sustained discussion. Use **On the side** when
they say they are busy, are dictating, or can answer only briefly. Infer On the side from a pattern
of short replies or a short reply plus an availability cue; one terse answer to a simple question
is only a cue to make the next turn easy to answer. Follow an explicit switch immediately. Adapt
quietly without adding a mode-selection question. Dictated text may not reveal that voice was used.

The mode changes question order, response length, and presentation. It does not change the goal,
the chosen skill, action authority, or a required human decision.

## Focused

Use the selected skill's normal depth. For an interview, work through independent material
questions in manageable rounds, then refine as answers expose dependent choices. Keep the user
oriented to what has been settled and why the next question matters.

## On the side

Read the request and available evidence first. Ask one short, high-level, decision-bearing
question whose answer would still help if the user leaves immediately. Choose the most
consequential unknown among desired outcome, proof of success, hard constraint, and what work may
continue while the user is away; do not re-ask what the request already settles. When two
independent unknowns are both essential, ask at most two short questions that can be answered in
one or two sentences. Put the most valuable one first.

After each answer, retain the usable decision, do agent-findable work, and ask the next narrower
question only if it still changes the work. Phrase a specific choice with a brief recommendation
that a short reply can accept or correct. Keep questions, interim updates, and final handoffs
short and easy to follow by voice. If transcription is ambiguous, restate the uncertain point
briefly; a vague "yes" confirms only the immediately preceding clear proposal.

If the user says "just go," use it as confirmation of a concrete proposal immediately before it
when the referent is clear. Otherwise continue already-authorized research and reversible work,
state consequential assumptions, and leave unanswered human choices visible. Ask again only when
the next action truly needs that choice or authority. Silence is not an answer. Preserve the mode,
settled decisions, and open questions in any continuity checkpoint.
